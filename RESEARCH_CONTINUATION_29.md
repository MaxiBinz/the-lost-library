**Date:** 25 October 2026. **Question:** do the primary Fragmenta catalogue records describe IV.156 and IV.157 as the same book, or as stylistically related manuscripts with separate proposed joins?

## Finding

The National Library of Finland's Fragmenta catalogue entries describe F.m.IV.156 and F.m.IV.157 as Dominican, Åbo-diocese antiphonaries with closely similar layout and notation. Both have one column and 11 lines; both use square notation on a red four-line staff; their dimensions and staff intervals overlap. The entries explicitly say that IV.156 bears a strong stylistic resemblance to IV.157. The catalogue dates them separately (about 1460–1475 and 1460–1486, respectively).

The records also use different relationship labels. IV.156 lists Turku Ms. c under “from same codex” and MPO Codex 1505 as “suggested.” IV.157 lists CCM Ant 99 and MPO Fr 1385 under “from same codex”; it presents Turku Ms. c and MPO Codex 1505 as suggested. In particular, the shared Codex 1505 suggestion is not itself a claim that IV.156 and IV.157 are one codex. The catalogue's strong stylistic resemblance claim is a broad relationship, not a physical join.

This gives a more precise reading of the E27 BOMPAC crosswalk and E28 Walta context: a broad stylistic/production group, catalogue suggestions, and same-codex associations can coexist without contradiction. It does not resolve the specific IV.173 candidate joins, establish a new attribution, or demonstrate that any one suggested leaf belongs to a target book.
