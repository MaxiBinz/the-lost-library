# E72 — a Barbara-office comparison for the Fr 8201 text

2 November 2026. Exploratory comparison selected after seeing the target text and source references. The observation below is prior art, not a newly discovered medieval composition.

## What the sources say

Grajewski 2022, p. 293, reports a St Catherine antiphon beginning *Specie corporis decora nimis enituit sed fide* on Fr 20106, and continuing *pulchrior sponso superno placuit* on Fr 8201. Our [existing photograph and annotation](research/continuation_65/observations.json) support a provisional reading of that ending on Fr 8201, followed by *Traditur ergo*; we have not seen Fr 20106. The pairing and Catherine context are Grajewski's published conclusions.

The machine-readable [Latin Medieval Liturgical Offices index, entry BA49](https://hlub.dyndns.org/projekten/webplek/CANTUS/cgi-bin/LMLO/LMLO.cgi?X=BA49&raw_text=true), describes an office for the **translation of St Barbara** and lists a similar *Specie corporis* passage: it has *erat decora nimis* rather than Grajewski's *decora nimis enituit*, but shares the ending *sed fide pulchrior sponso superno placuit*. The index places a Barbara version of *Nobilis et pulchra* immediately after it as the next antiphon, not as the later Catherine responsory visible on 8201. Its recorded source is **Analecta Hymnica 52 (1909), no. 386, pp. 334–337**. The web search index exposed the BA49 data; a direct open timed out and was not repeated.

We also consulted an [online text extraction of the 1909 edition](https://de.scribd.com/doc/66837361/Dreves-Analecta-Hymnica-Medii-Aevi-1886-Volume-LII), pp. 334 and 337. Although its OCR mixes printed columns and contains errors, it independently exposes the Barbara heading, the two similar chant openings and the editor's manuscript attribution. A [Fondazione Levi source index](https://www.fondazionelevi.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/AH-source-index.pdf), entry 2647, identifies the edited manuscript as a 15th-century collection from the Celestines of Amiens, then in the Rosenthal private collection. Its present location and the medieval original were not checked. **The database, extraction and source index are representations of the same edited witness, not three independent manuscript witnesses.** We have not inspected the original printed page image.

## What it changes—and does not change

The ending on Fr 8201 is not specific to a Catherine office: the same words also occur in the reported Barbara office. This gives a concrete alternative to test when identifying an isolated ending. But the **different earlier wording**, the different subsequent sequence and the explicit Catherine context reported by Grajewski matter. The Barbara example does not prove that the Swedish chant is for Barbara, that the Swedish fragments are unrelated, or that the published interpretation is wrong. It is not an independently verified reading of the Swedish fragment or of the Amiens manuscript.

The MPO catalogue's separate report of Barbara material and the hymn *Novo decurrens lumine* on Fr 20106 does not say that this particular Barbara translation office is present. Nor does this comparison establish that office's date of celebration, physical location in Fr 20106, or a connection to Amiens.
