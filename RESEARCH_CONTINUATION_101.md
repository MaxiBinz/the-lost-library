# E101 — a contemporary link between Szilágyi and the Sziget Lyceum

**3 November 2026.** This follow-up to E67–E68 was selected after the 1399 breviary candidate was known. It adds historical context, not an identification of manuscripts.

## Observation

Balázs Szöllősi's 1864 profile, *Vasárnapi Újság* no. 27, p. 261 onward, says that István Szilágyi came to Máramaros in 1845 as a teacher at the Reformed lyceum. It describes the same institution as later reduced to a gymnasium and subsequently restored as a higher school. It also says that the school library grew during his tenure and that he spent much of his time there. The passage was read in a [modern transcription with omissions](https://deske.hu/iras/berszangabor/a-human/05-hum/h12-21.htm); the [national-library bibliography](https://mek.oszk.hu/12500/12586/pdf/12586_2-1.pdf) identifies the 1864 issue and author. The original print image was not checked in this pass. An independently published [history of the Máramarossziget Reformed Lyceum](https://biblioteca-digitala.ro/reviste/Revista-Arhivei-Maramuresene/03_Revista-Arhivei-Maramuresene_03_2010.pdf), read through a search-indexed extract, also calls Szilágyi a teacher at the lyceum from 1845. These sources speak to the institution and person, not to the medieval manuscript.

Separately, [Gábriel's 1942 description](https://epa.oszk.hu/00000/00021/00211/pdf/MKSZ_EPA00021_1942_66_04_00211.pdf) says the Máramarossziget Lyceum deposited a fourteenth-century breviary, historical no. 167, at Debrecen; he reports a 1399 completion inscription. E67 established that [Toldy](https://mek.oszk.hu/17000/17077/17077.pdf), writing in 1855, linked a 1399 Sziget breviary to Szilágyi. See E100 for the exact earlier article he cites and its access limit.

## Inference and limits

The 1864 profile strengthens the contextual connection between the person behind Toldy's citation and the institution Gábriel names: Szilágyi was a teacher at a Máramarossziget lyceum and was associated with its library. Matching the historical institutions across the sources remains an interpretation; neither the profile nor the 2010 extract independently traces ownership of no. 167 in 1850. Szöllősi's account concerns the situation by 1864, and it does not establish that Szilágyi consulted this particular medieval book. Nor does the reported 1399 date establish that no. 167 and Toldy's source are the same object: the reports could depend on one another, contain errors, or describe different manuscripts. The original Szilágyi article and both medieval books remain uninspected.

The direct test is still an authoritative object-level cross-reference, matching specific codicological details or a comparison of the cited Catherine passage and colophon in properly identified witnesses. Neither this institutional context nor a shared chant variant establishes a physical connection with Fr 8201. No new attribution, scientific novelty, independent expert review or discovery is claimed.
