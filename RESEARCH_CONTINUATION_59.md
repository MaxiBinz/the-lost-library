## What the chapter adds

[Hilkka-Liisa Vuori and Marika Räsänen's 2025 peer-reviewed chapter](https://researchportal.helsinki.fi/fi/publications/rosa-rorans-bonitatem-antifoni-turun-hiippakunnan-nuottik%C3%A4sikirjo/) examines the *Rosa rorans* office in Finnish sources, including F.m. IV.156. We read selected web-reader text and figure captions from printed pp. 183–187 and 205–221, particularly pp. 208–209, 211–218 and 220–221, in the [publisher's book](https://oa.finlit.fi/fi/books/184/files/5199443d-0be7-4f42-aaa1-425922b42d19.pdf). This was not a full reading of the chapter. Finnish interpretation is AI-assisted and has not been checked by an independent translator. The University of Helsinki record gives the publication date as 25 November 2025 and the licence as CC BY-NC-ND.

The authors describe an unusually extended St Birgitta Vespers in IV.156 relative to their surviving Finnish comparisons. They argue against several proposed contexts of use and propose that a side altar at Turku Cathedral is a possible setting. That is **their published historical interpretation**, not our discovery or an independently established use place. They report Taitto's and Keskiaho's earlier work on possible Naantali production, and explicitly point out that where a book was made need not be where it was used. Their reported summary is not a replacement for reading those earlier works, and the 2025 chapter's interpretation should not be silently transferred to Fr 8994 while its book association remains unresolved.

The chapter also describes a useful reading hazard: at IV.156's *Rosa* opening, the authors regard an apparent extra note in an earlier transcription as ink showing through from the reverse. They report other small differences in selected musical passages and interpret them as normal variation. These are their observations and interpretations; we did **not** inspect that note independently, and this does not resolve the separate uncertain pitches in our E39/E55 study.
