# E84: the Turku records' photographs agree

**E85 viewer note:** In a subsequent session, the record reset an attempted deep link to the first image. To see the other face, use the viewer's second-image control; exact image label and manual navigation are in [E85](RESEARCH_CONTINUATION_85.md).

2 November 2026. The [museum record TKM23245:3](https://museo.disec.fi/Yksa4/id/160988864803200/#tab/overview) and [Codices Fennici item 249](https://www.codicesfennici.fi/items/show/249#?c=&m=&s=&cv=0&xywh=-388%2C-347%2C7748%2C5396) display the same fragment face, or a very close reproduction: both show the large red **O** in the right writing field, the same uneven upper-left edge, the central fold, and the same arrangement of notation and text. This strengthens the proposed **record-to-record crosswalk** beyond matching text copied from one description. The museum overview image is small; shared photographic provenance and a possible image-attachment error mean this is not independent confirmation of the physical object or its history.

The [other Codices Fennici viewer image](https://www.codicesfennici.fi/items/show/249#?c=&m=&s=&cv=1&xywh=0%2C-63%2C6984%2C4864) shows the opposite photographed face, with a large blue initial in its right writing field and a faint later inscription below. Both displayed faces have two text fields divided by a central fold, consistent with the TEI description of a damaged bifolium. This project viewing is reference-exposed and does not independently establish medieval side names, quire position, or the exact physical measurements. Marks at the fold were not measured or compared to the Helsinki leaves.

The practical gain is that a reviewer can now inspect the two public records and original viewer directly: **the museum's object count of one can describe one object with two folios**, as the Codices Fennici record states. This does not resolve whether the separately described provincial-archive Ms. c is the same object, or whether the intended Helsinki reference is IV.156 or IV.157. The IV.156 handlist association and E83's alternatives remain in force. This is not a new codex attribution, verified shelfmark correction, or claim of a historical discovery.
