# E103 — a different ordering of the same Catherine chants

**3 November 2026.** Exploratory comparison selected after Fr 8201 and its published comparators were known; not an independent test set or a new reconstruction candidate.

**Later qualification:** [E104](RESEARCH_CONTINUATION_104.md) identifies a plausible difference in liturgical structure and previously published sources with the shorter order. The order contrast must not be treated as a distinctive identity signature.

## Observations

The existing [Fr 8201 photograph](research/continuation_65/images/Fr8201-capture-1-native.jpg) shows the end of *Specie corporis*, then *Traditur ergo*, followed locally by the red initial of *Nobilis et pulchra*. Short rubrics and cues also appear between the chants. The first chant begins outside the preserved visible region; the readings are exposed to published references and not independently checked. The photograph and the region selectors are documented in [E65](RESEARCH_CONTINUATION_65.md) and [E66](RESEARCH_CONTINUATION_66.md).

The search-indexed [Cantus entry and folio navigation](https://cantusdatabase.org/chant/232925/) describe **Einsiedeln, Stiftsbibliothek, Codex 611(89)**, ff. 247v–248r. The two antiphons *Specie corporis* (204720) and *Traditur ergo* (204938) are adjacent in that index, but three additional antiphons occur before *Nobilis* (601492): *Cum esset adhuc in annis* (201006), *Maxentius instat impius* (203060), and *Caesar electos convocat rhetores* (200717). Cantus transcribes the opening of *Nobilis* there as *virgo … puella* and *vaga*, contrasting with Fr 8201's reference-exposed provisional *virgo … beata* and *vaga*. The [e-codices manuscript description](https://www.e-codices.ch/en/searchresult/list/one/sbe/0611) identifies this as an Einsiedeln antiphoner, dated prior to 1314 in that description.

## Interpretation

This indexed manuscript is a useful **contrast**, not a blind negative control: the two opening antiphons and all three chant identities can occur in a different local order. Thus, identifying the three chants without preserving their immediate order would obscure a difference that the existing Fr 8201 image allows us to investigate. The variant *vaga* alone is not unique; a second word, *puella*, also differs in the indexed Einsiedeln text. The data do not establish how common either sequence or reading is.

The Einsiedeln folio images were not inspected. Cantus's inventory can contain indexing, omission or folio-navigation errors, and the Swedish reading is not independently reviewed. This comparison does not decide whether Fr 20106 and Fr 8201 came from the same book, establish a connection to the Sziget breviary, or make a chronological, melodic, geographic or manuscript-identity claim. It is a check against overinterpreting shared chant identifiers, not a claim of scientific novelty. The frozen queue and formal model evaluation are unchanged.
