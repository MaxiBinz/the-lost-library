## The concrete comparison

The [Finnish National Library's indexed IV.156 description](https://fragmenta.kansalliskirjasto.fi/handle/10024/1299?show=full) describes folio 8 as containing St James material and ending at *intercede* in the antiphon *O beate Iacobe*. The available text is an indexed catalogue transcription, not our inspection of the Finnish leaf; the exact wording, leaf edge and original surface need checking.

On the retained [Fr 10406 photograph 1](data/images/mpo-10406.jpg), a James heading accompanies an apparent *O beate Iacobe* passage. Its text continues provisionally *intercede pro nobis ad dominum*, followed by a psalm-ending cue and the beginning of *O lux et decus*. Exact pixels, dimensions and hashes are in [observations.json](research/continuation_112/observations.json). This Swedish image reading is not independently reviewed. The indexed Finnish description names a specific work and boundary word corresponding to the Swedish reading, but the unexplained bracket means the Finnish surviving letters are not established.

If both pieces actually preserve the same letters of *intercede*, both refer to the same unique occurrence in one continuous book, and the Helsinki and Swedish pieces are distinct physical objects, a simple non-overlapping reconstruction would require revision. Those premises are not established. In particular, the indexed Finnish description does not show which letters survive, and the catalogue or Swedish reading could be wrong; source identity, physical extent or side mapping could be misunderstood; the same chant might have been copied more than once or occur in a composite volume. We have not inspected IV.156 f.8 or established that the surviving physical ink overlaps. The reported text alone cannot distinguish these possibilities.

## A useful local sequence, with a counterexample

On Fr 10406, photograph 1's *O lux* passage appears to break *a- / postolos* across photographs 1 and 2. Photograph 2 ends provisionally in *om-*, and photograph 3 begins *nium[que?] populorum* before an Olaf heading. These matches support a **proposed local textual order 1 → 2 → 3** if each image shows the same occurrence; they do not prove a physical seam, complete original side map or absence of a St Anne office elsewhere. Reading from the small images and the rubric require independent checks.

The [2011 Daroca catalogue, fragment 136](https://ifc.dpz.es/recursos/publicaciones/30/73/114fragmento136.pdf), pp. 829–830, describes both *O beate Iacobe* and *O lux et decus* in a separately catalogued antiphonary leaf, dated there to the second half of the sixteenth century. We read its transcription and description, not its manuscript photographs. It shows that the **two-chant pairing is not unique** to the Swedish fragment. Its service labels and period are not assumed to match the Swedish witness, and it does not identify the Finnish leaf or settle the overlapping-word question.

## Next decisive test

Compare a securely labelled image of **both sides of IV.156 f.8** with Fr 10406's first photograph at *O beate Iacobe*: assess the exact word, line break, music and ink features; verify the catalogue's ending and both objects' physical extent. Different ink for the same unique occurrence would challenge a simple single-copy assignment, subject to repeated-office and composite alternatives. The same distinctive ink would instead demand an object-identity or image/catalogue crosswalk investigation. A different or unreadable Finnish passage leaves this test unresolved. Compare the full St James and St Olaf context before using the proposed sequence to argue about St Anne.
