# Swedish targets, existing work, and legal comparison

Phase 0 research checked 19 October 2026. Findings are evidence for planning, not new manuscript identifications. Primary URLs below were read through web research; one legal IIIF manifest and one small JPEG were also actually retrieved and the image inspected.

## 1. CODICUM overlaps directly with the proposed ambition

[CODICUM About](https://www.codicum.eu/about) dates the project 1 April 2025–31 March 2031 and identifies more than 50,000 Nordic parchment fragments. Objective 7 explicitly concerns connecting fragments across collections and visualising reconstructed books in a virtual library. Biomolecular and visual methods are combined with palaeography and history. Consequently neither Nordic cross-collection reconstruction nor a virtual library is a defensible independent novelty claim.

Actual published resources include the [Nordic Survey](https://www.codicum.eu/resources/nordic-survey), assembled from catalogues and correspondence. The [resources page](https://www.codicum.eu/resources) says its 2025 survey expanded identified holding institutions from 42 to nearly 120 and that CODICUM updated the MNLL wiki. These are resources already delivered, distinct from future book identifications.

Riksarkivet's dated [5 May 2026 technical account](https://riksarkivet.se/resurser/codicum-sticker-ut) describes a virtual reconstruction tool entering prototype development, reusing partner systems and IIIF. It also reports AI-assisted conversion of Danish physical catalogues followed by scholarly correction. Do not repeat the page's promotional claim of unprecedented tooling without independent assessment against Fragmentarium and other viewers.

The dated [conservation account](https://riksarkivet.se/resurser/provtagning-pagar) reports photography and sampling of approximately 23,000 Swedish fragments planned across 2026–2027, beginning January 2026, including previously unpictured objects. It describes ZooMS plus complementary DNA analyses. This demonstrates ongoing work and prospect of improved coverage, not completion by today. Both Riksarkivet pages timed out through web `open`; their complete institutional text was retrieved by targeted web search.

[CODICUM news](https://www.codicum.eu/news) describes Helsinki sampling beginning October 2025, a 2026 Leeds session, and WP3 research identifying Dominican-related fragments c.1250–1450. Check coordination before selecting a Dominican book as supposedly neglected.

## 2. Important additional existing integration work

Seppo Eskola, [Fragments Combined](https://www.fragmentology.ms/article/download/RN_Eskola_08/8737/24725), Fragmentology VIII (2025), 233–240, DOI [10.24446/no5r](https://doi.org/10.24446/no5r), describes BOMPAC's harmonized Stockholm–Helsinki corpus and early modern reuse metadata. Its dataset is [Zenodo 17511106](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17511106); accompanying raw data is [17701360](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17701360). It reports 29,230 objects, including printed fragments, and explains why leaves, physical fragments, catalogue records and reconstructed codices cannot be counted interchangeably. It explicitly warns of inconsistent institutional metadata, duplicate entries, overlapping codex sigla and missing digital images. It treats the export as research data rather than a replacement catalogue. Existing corpus harmonization and provenance analysis are therefore prior art.

The other directly relevant primary study is Jaakko Tahkokallio, [Fragments Re-Connected](https://www.doria.fi/bitstream/handle/10024/187483/Tahkokallio_Fragments-Reconnected.pdf?sequence=1), Mirator 23:1 (2023), 1–29, DOI [10.54334/mirator.v23i1.125301](https://doi.org/10.54334/mirator.v23i1.125301). It publishes cross-collection twelfth-/early-thirteenth-century missal associations; these must be excluded from discovery claims and can supply benchmark associations. Its introductory note says MPO fragments registered as Kungliga biblioteket are physically at Riksarkivet: distinguish old collection labels from current location.

## 3. Strong Swedish target: contested Ant 49 cluster

**Object identity:** This is a well-defined catalogue cluster spanning at least two books, not an authenticated single codex or a single manuscript family in the palaeographic sense.

- Type/date/origin: antiphonaries; fifteenth-century dating for Fr 8201 and 8304, fourteenth-century catalogue date for 20106; Germany/Baltic/Sweden are competing tentative catalogue origins. Do not harmonize away the contradiction.
- Exact identifiers: CCM Ant 49; MPO Codex 1146 includes Fr 8201 and 8304; Fr 20106 is separately registered in collection KB A 103, k 95. All are managed by Riksarkivet according to current export and the location caution above.
- Extent in the root's retrieved 2025 export: exact `ccmsignum == Ant 49` gives 8 rows: **8066, 8072, 8073, 8201, 8304, 9620, 20106, 20107**. Seven rows have nonempty manifest URLs. This is **seven image pointers**, not seven images individually verified by this subtask. There may be physical duplicates or reassigned members.
- Sample viewer: [Fr 8201](https://sok.riksarkivet.se/bildvisning/R1008201); [Fr 8304](https://sok.riksarkivet.se/bildvisning/R1008304). Corresponding manifests: [8201](https://lbiiif.riksarkivet.se/arkis!R1008201/manifest), [8304](https://lbiiif.riksarkivet.se/arkis!R1008304/manifest). Root is testing these. Fr 20106 lacks image/manifest fields in this export.
- Existing correction: Czesław Grajewski, [Offices of St Catherine of Alexandria](https://czasopisma.uni.opole.pl/index.php/ls/article/download/4818/4236/13686), DOI [10.25167/ls.4818](https://doi.org/10.25167/ls.4818), p.293, argues that 20106 and 8201 continue the same chant text, while 8304 belongs to another book despite apparently sharing the hand. Repeating that correction is replication, not discovery. The PDF header reads “Liturgia Sacra 29 (2022), nr 1”; cite DOI to avoid silently correcting ambiguous volume/year typography.
- What remains uncertain: whether the other catalogue-group members should remain with either reconstructed book; whether any extra fragments survive elsewhere; current curator assessment after 2022. The absence of literature fields does not make these questions unknown to specialists.
- Possible AI value: generate ranked comparisons across the remaining members, with text order and duplicated office positions checking visual similarity. This tests the valuable distinction between same hand and same book.
- Successful contribution: a curator-reviewed membership audit, justified exclusions and/or an additional supported leaf attribution; a negative result with reproducible evidence is still useful.
- Public artifact: side-by-side images, office sequences, accepted/rejected group edges, dates and literature provenance with uncertainty visible.
- Main risk: a genuine known positive uses an undigitized leaf; same-scribe overlap defeats naive embeddings; catalogue membership cannot be treated as truth.

**Secondary control:** Ant 205, Fr 20902 and 20900, is supported as a text-continuity pair in the same Grajewski article p.296. Exact export cluster has 3 rows (20900, 20901, 20902) and zero manifests. It is not currently an image-ready benchmark.

## 4. Other Swedish music leads: use with limits

[Scattered Songs in Scandinavia](https://cas-nor.no/index.php/yc-project/scattered-songs-scandinavia), a 2020/21 CAS project led by Catherine Bradley, identifies Riksarkivet Fr 5786 and Fr 813 as three surviving bifolia (12 pages) from one Parisian motet manuscript, late thirteenth/early fourteenth century. This is a specific surviving book with a compelling cross-border story and established same-book relation. It is polyphonic motets, outside the requested antiphonary/gradual focus; its recent dedicated project makes an independent novelty claim particularly weak. Its digitized extent was not verified here; do not promote it as image-ready.

Gunilla Björkvall's [Liturgical Sequences](https://vitterhetsakademien.ekvist.se/doi-1xxglp), DOI [10.62077/1xxglp](https://doi.org/10.62077/1xxglp), already inventories and reconstructs Swedish sequentiary, gradual and missal sources. The catalogue is essential prior art before proposing a sequence-based reconstruction as original. Publisher pages give a 2016 publication date, while other references cite 2015; preserve the precise edition citation used.

## 5. Nordic legal direction: a verified comparison, not a better novelty case

[NordicLaw, University of Bergen](https://www4.uib.no/en/research/research-projects/nordic-law-books-the-production-and-use-of-vernacular-law-manuscripts-in-the-north-from-1100-to-1600-nordiclaw) is explicitly researching law manuscripts and fragments c.1100–1600 across Iceland, Norway, Denmark and Sweden, with codicology, philology, history and art history. The displayed duration is January 2023–December 2026; other funding accounts date particular grants 2024–2026. This is active overlap, not an untouched field.

**Concrete comparison object:** [Kungliga biblioteket B 193](https://www.manuscripta.se/ms/100332), Äldre Västgötalagen fragment, Västergötland 1200–1250, two Old Swedish parchment leaves, one hand, 12 written lines; formerly reused around Norwegian accounts for Øvre Telemarken 1638 and transferred to KB in 1862. Catalogue description **CC BY 4.0**, images **Public Domain Mark**. Its old editions go back to Klemming, Friesen and Wessén. The catalogue does not establish any unidentified companion leaf.

Actual technical tests:

- Manifest [100332](https://www.manuscripta.se/iiif/100332/manifest.json): HTTP 200, application/json, **37,582 bytes**.
- **18 canvases**, but only **four actual medieval leaf sides**: f.1r,1v,2r,2v. The remainder are covers, old binding, and related views. Counting canvases as independent fragments would be wrong.
- [f.1r JPEG at 800px width](https://iiif.manuscripta.se/iiif/ms-100332/ms-100332_0003.tif/full/800,/0/default.jpg): HTTP 200, image/jpeg, **186,621 bytes**, saved and visually inspected. The dark, worn leaf is image-accessible but not straightforward OCR material.
- Saved manifest: `research/legal_b193_manifest.json`; receipt incl. SHA256/license: `research/legal_b193_probe.json`; actual JPEG: `research/evidence/legal_b193_f1r.jpg`.

Inference: legal materials can offer excellent catalogue/licensing/IIIF infrastructure and avoid music recognition, but they still need Old Swedish/Old Norse/Latin specialists, face same-text different-book confusion and active competing scholarship. B193 is a concrete access demonstration; a missing companion relationship has **not** been established. Do not recommend switching to law solely because the image works.

## 6. Relevant technical prior work

- [Cross-codex Learning for Reliable Scribe Identification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04296), Weißmann et al. (2023), demonstrates codex-related overfitting and motivates cross-codex separation, masking and a reject option. Its reported scribe classification is not a demonstrated same-book matcher for Nordic music.
- [REPERTORIUM](https://repertorium.eu/) already develops AI/OMR/music retrieval and a large chant platform. [ÖAW's project](https://www.oeaw.ac.at/acdh/research/musicology/research/documentation-source-studies/ai-assisted-music-notation-recognition) develops OMR for late medieval plainchant. Applying OMR to damaged, cropped, mixed-neume Nordic fragments still requires validation.
- [DigiPal/ScandiPal project code](https://github.com/kcl-ddh/digipal) and [project account](https://digipal.huma-num.fr/blog/digipal-at-dh2014/) establish longstanding structured handwriting comparisons for Scandinavian fragments. Do not claim the first digital palaeographic fragment comparison.

## Recommendation to root

Conditional GO for a small externally reviewed replication and retrieval pilot, preferably using an image-ready Finnish antiphonary/gradual cross-collection group. Include Ant49 as a deliberately difficult catalogue-audit/hard-negative control. Novelty is conditional until current BOMPAC/CODICUM work and bibliographies are checked. Legal images are usable but no stronger unresolved target was verified here. Do not count the Ant49 correction, published cross-border joins, existing combined export or a virtual library concept as this project's discoveries.
