## Candidate 1 — Graduale Aboense, F.m.II.44 (single reconstructed book)

**Catalog facts.** [Full record](https://fragmenta.kansalliskirjasto.fi/handle/10024/1610?show=full), URN:NBN:fi-fd2011-fra0413. Gradual, Åbo/Turku liturgical use; dated 1397–1406 in the updated note, versus broad structured date 1301–1500. Helsinki holds 48 catalogued folios, with 96 individual TIFF sides listed in the live record. Published same-codex association: MPO Codex 1104 = CCM Sequ 42, Stockholm Riksarkivet, Fr 7957, 7960, 7961, 8343, 8344, 9242, 28175 (seven fragment records, fourteen folios). Thus 62 catalog-associated leaves across institutions, not a verified 62-leaf downloadable inventory. Catalog marks images Public Domain Mark 1.0. [Listed sample TIFF](https://fragmenta.kansalliskirjasto.fi/bitstream/handle/10024/1610/F_m_II_44-0001.tif?sequence=1&isAllowed=y), 61.49 MB, not downloaded. Taitto's 2002 *Graduale Aboense 1397–1406* is an established partial reconstruction; [publisher record](https://kirjat.finlit.fi/EN/page/product/graduale-aboense-1397-1406/21983?category=4949). Its historical Finnish interlinear additions are already studied. See also [Hannikainen 2017](https://taju.uniarts.fi/bitstream/10024/6010/1/TA2017-4-04Hannikainen.pdf), pp. 324–327.

**Proposed research and contribution (inference).** Best local positive-control book: hide Stockholm links and test recovery against similar Åbo graduals. A genuinely additional leaf could improve a well-studied reconstruction and the account-reuse history. Public artifact could expose surviving chant sequence and reuse annotations with every link attributed. No specific additional leaf is identified in Phase 0; novelty requires comparison with Taitto, MPO, CODICUM and unpublished current work. **20 October correction:** all seven Stockholm records exist in the export. Fr 28175 has CCM Sequ 42 and two leaves but blank codex/image fields, so the earlier exact-codex extraction omitted it. This was a filtering error, not an absent record or new fragment. [Direct evidence](goal_identity_crosscheck.json).

## Candidate 2 — F.m.IV.157, St Catherine antiphonal (single catalogued book; boundaries disputed)

**Catalog facts.** [Full record](https://fragmenta.kansalliskirjasto.fi/handle/10024/1300?show=full), URN:NBN:fi-fd2011-fra0852. Dominican–Åbo antiphonal, probably Nådendal; heading ca. 1460–1486, updated Keskiaho 2010 note ca. 1460–1475. Helsinki: fourteen folios, twenty-eight TIFF sides; one column, eleven lines. Existing same-codex attributions: CCM Ant 99 = Riksarkivet Fr 20274, 20275, 20276, 20277, 20278 (five leaves), plus MPO Fr 1385 (one leaf). Explicitly suggested: Turku Ms. c and MPO Codex 1505 = Fr 8994, 10370, 10406, 10407, 10437; these are also suggested under IV.156. [Listed sample TIFF](https://fragmenta.kansalliskirjasto.fi/bitstream/handle/10024/1300/F_m_IV_157-0001.tif), 73.20 MB, untested. The catalog contains Taitto's discussion and distinguishes resemblance, suggestion and same-codex assertions. Public Domain Mark 1.0. Count: fourteen Helsinki digitized leaves verified at listing level; six further catalog-associated leaves and six suggested leaves are not an audited image inventory.

**Proposed research and contribution (inference).** Strongest narrowly unresolved candidate: reassess which of the existing disputed leaves fit this book versus IV.156, comparing opening signatures, text, hands, ruling and original sequence. AI could organize contradictory evidence and retrieve additional lookalikes. A success might be a defensible correction or rejection, not necessarily a new join. Artifact: annotated competing reconstructions with uncertain leaves movable between hypotheses. Obstacles: extensive damage, unfinished musical sections, same-workshop similarity and the possibility that specialists have already settled catalog ambiguities offline. Do not advertise recovering these listed associations as discovery.

## Candidate 3 — F.m.IV.160 (single book and cross-border positive control)

**Catalog facts.** [Full record](https://fragmenta.kansalliskirjasto.fi/handle/10024/1303?show=full), URN:NBN:fi-fd2011-fra0855. Dominican–Åbo antiphonal; fifteenth century, updated to its second half; probably Nådendal/Åbo. Helsinki: ten folios, twenty TIFF sides listed. Explicit same-codex link: CCM Ant 140 = Stockholm Riksarkivet Fr 20340, Finska cameralia 88:3:3, Åbo 1615 Införingslängd (one fragment, one leaf). Eleven catalog-associated leaves overall; Swedish digitization not independently audited here. One column, nine lines, 21.5–22.5 × 33.5–34.5 cm, red four-line staves, 19–20 mm. Damaged partial sixth folio. [Listed sample TIFF](https://fragmenta.kansalliskirjasto.fi/bitstream/handle/10024/1303/F_m_IV_160-0001.tif?sequence=1&isAllowed=y), 136.3 MB, not downloaded. Public Domain Mark 1.0. The record cites Maliniemi, Taitto 1992 and earlier reproductions and points to the related Åbo/Nådendal production group.

**Proposed research and contribution (inference).** Hide Fr 20340 to test realistic cross-institution recovery. Additional candidate leaves must have nine-line ruling compatible with this book, not merely the eleven-line appearance of IV.156/157. A public reconstruction could explain how known Swedish and Finnish leaves preserve the same book, then separately show any new hypothesis. Main risks: very few held-out positives, color/scan differences, common chant text and confusing shared production with codex identity. Its absent leaves are not evidence that an undiscovered additional fragment survives.

## Candidate 4 — F.m.IV.28 and its explicitly bounded comparison family

**Catalog facts.** [Full record](https://fragmenta.kansalliskirjasto.fi/handle/10024/1232?show=full), URN:NBN:fi-fd2011-fra0723. Fourteenth-century secular Nordic antiphonal, second half suggested; Åbo excluded and several Swedish dioceses considered unlikely. Helsinki: thirty folios and sixty listed TIFF sides, two columns, fifteen lines, 15.5–16 × 22 cm; quadratic notation on red four-line staves. Related catalog units IV.29, IV.30, IV.36 and VII.85 are comparisons, not automatically one lost codex. Crucial explicit different-codex example: Stockholm Fr 10349 / CCM Ant 111 must not be joined to IV.28; overlapping contents contradict the attribution despite stylistic similarity. [Listed sample TIFF](https://fragmenta.kansalliskirjasto.fi/bitstream/handle/10024/1232/F_m_IV_28-0001.tif?sequence=1&isAllowed=y), 54.90 MB, untested. Public Domain Mark 1.0. Literature references include Taitto's catalog, Toy 1981 and Nilsson 2000. Count of digitized comparison-family leaves was not audited.

**Proposed research and contribution (inference).** Best identified local hard-negative family: test whether a visual matcher rejects the documented false relation, while distinguishing same script/production from same book. A successful future correction would require evidence stronger than resemblance. Artifact: comparison view displaying why plausible lookalikes fail. Main risks: family is not one codex; neither a specific new leaf nor a presently open scholarly controversy has been established.

## Scope recommendation

Narrow the initial music task to square-notation, fifteenth-century Åbo-related graduals/antiphonals, with specific book-level controls. This makes physical ruling, musical notation, liturgical sequencing and documented cross-border links comparable. The late-fifteenth-century Nådendal production group is a manuscript family, not one reconstructable book. Its catalog entry [F.m.IV.173](https://fragmenta.kansalliskirjasto.fi/handle/10024/1314) explicitly discusses eighteen manuscripts and alternative production explanations; count neither all those references as same-codex joins nor IV.156/157/173 as three independent validated discovery opportunities.

A credible first sequence is: validate on Graduale Aboense and IV.160; include IV.28–Fr 10349 as a difficult negative; only then investigate IV.157's disputed attribution neighborhood. This is conditional on access and a specialist establishing which uncertainties remain open as of 2026.

## Relevant pre-existing scholarship and provenance caveats

- [National Library collection description](https://www.kansalliskirjasto.fi/en/collections/fragmenta-membranea-collection): about 9,300 leaves from 1,500 medieval books; realm-wide accounts once stored in Stockholm and Finland-related records moved to Helsinki after 1809. Present holding location and account origin therefore do not securely establish medieval manuscript origin.
- [Taitto 2001 catalog bibliographic record](https://data.nationallibrary.fi/bib/me/W00057022000), IV:1 text and IV:2 plates; a necessary prior source for antiphonal relationships. The indexed academic survey [Latinankielinen kirkkolaulu ja sen lähteet](https://muhi.uniarts.fi/latinankielinen-kirkkolaulu/) describes 898 antiphonal leaves assigned to 203 books in Taitto's catalog; this historical catalog count differs from current site's 210 `Antiphonal` records and must not be silently equated.
- [Hannikainen 2017](https://taju.uniarts.fi/bitstream/10024/6010/1/TA2017-4-04Hannikainen.pdf), pp. 324–328: shared liturgical tradition can preserve nearly identical texts and melodies across separate sources; variants may illuminate tradition, without proving a physical book identity. He specifically identifies F.m.II.44 as Graduale Aboense, and calls for broader musical comparison. Do not turn his discussion into an unqualified 2026 research gap.
- No curator or CODICUM researcher was contacted. No undocumented relationship has been established. Bibliographic records and catalog suggestions establish prior knowledge and ambiguity; they do not establish novelty.
