The strongest **technical positive control** remains **Graduale Aboense, F.m.II.44 / Codex 1104 / CCM Sequ 42**. These are different priorities. A successful recovery of its known leaves is validation, not an original discovery.

The defensible decision is **CONDITIONAL GO**. The record supports a precise catalogue-level question, accessible comparison material and a credible falsification strategy. It does not establish that the question is still unresolved in current specialist knowledge, nor does it prove any new same-codex association.

## Evidence that changes the next action

### 1. The catalogue language needs interpretation before membership scoring

The indexed [F.m.IV.156 full record](https://fragmenta.kansalliskirjasto.fi/handle/10024/1299?show=full), retrieved through a focused primary-source search on this review, qualifies the Codex1505 association and points to IV.157. Elsewhere in the same additional-information field it equates that group with IV.157. Thus a cross-reference cannot automatically be converted into either a secure positive label or a novel competing attribution. The wording supports investigating the grouping, but not presupposing the resolution. Direct `web.open` returned a cache miss; the source's indexed full metadata was readable. That access distinction should be preserved.

The source also names **Turku, Turun maakunta-arkisto, Ms.c**, one uncatalogued folio, as a related witness. Its present repository/shelfmark is not verified here. Do not infer present access from this older institutional name.

This review does not establish whether the apparent tension comes from genuinely competing scholarly assignments, shorthand for a shared production group, accumulated catalogue updates, or duplicate/inconsistent reconstructed-codex boundaries. Those explanations are hypotheses to discriminate.

### 2. The Swedish group is small enough for a concrete first comparison

I re-read the actual cached [MPO CSV](mpo.csv), not just report summaries. All five Codex1505 rows are one-leaf fifteenth-century antiphonals with `Quadr` notation, `#11` musical lines and manifest URLs. Their written spaces are **34.0–36.0 cm high and22.5–23.0 cm wide**. Leaf dimensions vary and may reflect trimming. These measurements make the group a tractable comparison target; they do not prove common manuscript identity.

The known IV.157 comparison rows **Fr1385 and20274–20278** are also present. Only1385 and20274 have manifest pointers. IV.160's Fr20340 has none. These differences justify ranking IV.160 below the image-supported alternatives, even though its known scholarly association is useful.

### 3. Neither missing pages nor blank catalogue fields create novelty

The [Graduale Aboense record](https://fragmenta.kansalliskirjasto.fi/handle/10024/1610?show=full) and Taitto's published reconstruction already establish the main cross-border relationship. Its incompleteness is not evidence that an unrecognized additional fragment survives in a searched collection. Likewise, the [published Ant49 correction](https://doi.org/10.25167/ls.4818) is prior art, even where the export has not incorporated it. Current unpublished CODICUM or curatorial knowledge remains outside this review.

## Concrete first investigation for the recommended family

**Research question:** What is the best supported relationship between each of Codex1505's five leaves and the independently described fragment groups IV.156 and IV.157, after prior same-book claims, text/chant positions and codicological constraints are separated from shared-production resemblance?

**Competing explanations to retain:**

1. The five leaves form a securely consistent part of one Helsinki book group.
2. Some members have been grouped too broadly and belong to another book.
3. The sources share production, hand or liturgical tradition but are different codices.
4. Apparent disagreement is catalogue shorthand, duplicated boundaries or a superseded statement, and no present unresolved attribution survives source reconciliation.

**Evidence collection before scoring:** obtain only small derivatives of the five Swedish leaves and selected representative sides from IV.156/157 plus known comparators1385/20274. Record physical-object/side IDs, native resolution, source URL and image-specific rights. Do not use the fourteen listed IV.157 folios as fourteen successfully fetched objects until bytes are actually verified. Stop at a documented access limitation if these essential comparisons cannot be obtained modestly.

**Falsification first:** map identified chant/text positions and overlapping content. If two supposed leaves occupy the same nonduplicated place in an office, explore whether that contradicts one-book identity before fitting visual similarity. Compare ruling, written-space dimensions, line/staff spacing and quire/folio evidence, preserving trimming and missing-data markers. Similarity is supporting evidence; a textual/codicological contradiction can outweigh it.

**Scholarly audit:** identify which author and date support each secure, qualified or rejected link, and check the current work before calling the question open. If existing research already settles the relation, record the result as replication or catalogue harmonization. This is a useful rejected hypothesis, not a failed project requiring an invented discovery.

**Bounded future result:** a membership evidence table for five Swedish fragments, source-linked acceptance/rejection/uncertainty for each hypothesis, and a list of any still-untested constraints. An eventual interactive artifact could show competing reconstructions, positions and contradictory evidence. Do not build a full reconstruction or public website during this goal.
