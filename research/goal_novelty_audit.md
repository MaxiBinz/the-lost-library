# Goal continuation: adversarial novelty audit of the Åbo–Nådendal antiphonals

**Research dates:** 19–20 October 2026, Europe/Vienna.  
**Scope:** primary catalogue and bibliography checks for F.m.IV.156, IV.157, IV.173, Swedish Codex 1505, and Turku Ms. c. No manuscript attribution, novelty claim, contact, paid acquisition, or matching model was made in this audit. Only this research note was edited by this subtask.

## Result

There is a **credible, specifically identified catalogue-level attribution question**, but the audit does **not** prove it remains unsettled in current scholarship. The strongest defensible proposal is an **attribution and exclusion audit of the family centred on F.m.IV.157**, rather than a claim that a newly noticed resemblance is a discovery.

An important missing piece of prior scholarship was identified: **Jesse Keskiaho, “En grupp handskrifter från Nådendal: nya rön,” pp. 51–67** in *Nådendal – Vallis Gratiae. Finlands Birgittakloster*, ISBN **978-91-88568-75-5**. The author's institutional bibliography dates the chapter **March 2019**; the publisher dates the volume **2018**. Its full text was **not obtained**, so neither its conclusions nor whether it resolves the catalogue suggestions may be asserted. This is a concrete novelty gate, not a reason to invent a scholarly gap.

## Attempts to disprove the promising interpretation

### 1. The relevant resemblance is not previously unnoticed

The Swedish Codex 1505 rows already cite Taitto's catalogue in connection with Ant 99 and Helsinki 156/157. The Finnish records already name both same-book and suggested relations. Consequently, an image matcher returning those IDs would be **replication or an evidential reassessment**, not discovery. The post-catalogue Keskiaho chapter raises an additional possibility that the apparent uncertainty has already been revisited or resolved. Without its content, a claim of current scholarly novelty is unproven.

### 2. A suggested Hy 49 relation has an observable metadata contradiction

The locally retrieved MPO row for **Fr 25592** gives:

- title **Hymnarium**;
- date **12.–13. Jh.**;
- **two columns, 35 lines**;
- square notation;
- [IIIF manifest pointer](https://lbiiif.riksarkivet.se/arkis!R1025592/manifest).

The IV.173 record assigns its leaf to the fifteenth century, updated to the second half, and places it in the late-medieval Åbo group. The chronology/layout mismatch is strong **evidence against treating the suggested relation as a positive gold label**. It is not yet proof that the fragment differs: an identifier crosswalk, erroneous date, differing catalogue line-count convention, or composite volume could explain a conflict. Inspect the actual image and confirm the alias before deciding. This audit did not retrieve Fr 25592's image; the parent investigation handles bounded target-image probes.

Similarly, **Fr 29666** has the accepted same-codex statement but sparse metadata, `Got` notation and `#9?` lines. Neither that row nor IV.173 should be blindly used to train a script/notation classifier. Source descriptions and image evidence need reconciliation.

### 3. Raw dimensions could create a false exclusion

IV.157's prose gives **22–23 × 33.5–35.5 cm** without a structured measurement-category field in the inspected table. The known-associated Fr 1385 has MPO `format` **\*43.5 × 32.0**, but written-space `schriftraum1` **34.0 × 23.0**. Codex 1505 leaf dimensions include **49.0 × 34.5** and written-space measurements around **35.5 × 22.5**.

The near transposition between Finnish prose dimensions and Swedish written-space figures is a reason to check the catalogues' conventions. It is **not** sufficient to declare any relation compatible or incompatible. Leaf size, written space, width/height order, trimming and unfolding must be resolved before a cross-collection dimension filter is applied. The old report's broad recommendation to use dimensions therefore needs an explicit harmonization gate.

### 4. Turku Ms. c is not yet a safely resolved physical object

The older IV.173 descriptive prose names **Turku Provincial Museum**, while the updated notes name **Turun maakunta-arkisto**. The older text describes a defective bifolium; the updated IV.157 note says one folio. This may be historical relocation, changing description, two objects or an error. The present repository, current shelfmark and physical extent were not independently established. Do not count these mentions as separate discovered leaves or silently choose a holder.

## Defensible unresolved research question and first falsification plan

**Question:** after checking the 2018/2019 scholarly update and resolving catalogue conventions, can opening signatures, chant positions, ruling, handwriting and notation distinguish **same codex** from **shared production** among the IV.157/Ant 99 group and its explicitly suggested IV.156, Codex 1505 and IV.173 comparisons?

This is evidence-backed as a *catalogue-level* question because exact objects and relation strengths are documented. It is conditional as an *original research opportunity* because current specialist conclusions are not known.

The smallest useful investigation should:

1. Read Keskiaho's later chapter and Taitto's cited pages, recording accepted, rejected and unresolved edges before running retrieval.
2. Resolve the Turku identity and measurement conventions. Keep unverified aliases and inherited group data out of gold labels and predictor inputs.
3. Inspect a few actual pages from IV.157/156, secure Ant 99 or Fr 1385 comparators, a Codex 1505 leaf, IV.173 and Fr 25592. Treat the last as a proposed falsification case until expert review; obtain Fr 29666 if it is to serve as a positive.
4. Ask whether text/opening-signature overlap rules out same-book identity before expensive visual comparison. Similar workshop style should survive as a possible relationship even when same-codex identity fails.
5. Use AI only to rank or organize comparisons. Any final attribution requires independent corroboration and a source-linked dossier, including evidence against it.

A successful result might be a defensible exclusion, a corrected crosswalk, or a strengthened attribution supported by multiple independent observations. An interactive artifact could show alternative leaf memberships and explain why a plausible visual match is accepted or rejected. It must not present an authenticated Nådendal book or newly recovered association unless the evidence actually supports that stronger claim.

**Recommendation to the parent:** maintain **CONDITIONAL GO**, sharpen the target into this bounded attribution audit, and make the unread 2018/2019 chapter an explicit first gate. If that source already settles the relevant edges, pivot to validating those published results or the next ranked candidate rather than rebranding them as novel.
