## First investigation and stop conditions

1. Read Keskiaho's **2018/2019** *En grupp handskrifter från Nådendal: nya rön*, pp.51–67, and Taitto's cited pages. The former's existence is verified, contents unread; publisher/author records differ by year. If it settles the edges, record replication or catalogue harmonization rather than claiming a new finding.
2. Make a relation ledger that keeps accepted, suggested, rejected, similar-production and unknown distinct. Do not merge IV.156/157 merely because a cross-reference equates them in one place.
3. Resolve measurement conventions, the Turku alias and physical object/side groupings. **Fr 28175 demonstrates that an exact-codex query can omit a known object even though the record exists.**
4. Compare a bounded set of representative sides. Existing small samples establish access; remaining views must be checked before counting staves, placing leaves or comparing full written areas.
5. Try to disprove same-book membership with text/chant overlap, duplicated office positions, ruling and opening signatures before interpreting visual resemblance.
6. Only after expert reference labels and independent validation are secured should visual retrieval rank additional candidates. No full reconstruction or public site is part of this goal.

**Separate falsification branch:** IV.173's note suggests **Hy 49 = Fr 25592**, but the MPO row dates that two-column hymn fragment to the twelfth/thirteenth century. Its actual image shows a very different layout from the large chant-page samples. This is a reason to reconcile the alias and reject it as automatic positive truth, not proof of an impossible relationship: catalogue error, an older reused unit or a composite book remain explanations. The known IV.173 positive **Fr 29666 / Ant 242** is documented but lacks an image link.

Ege/Beauvais remains the best supplementary positive-control route; legal material has demonstrated access but no stronger original question was found here. See the three-direction comparison in the report. No specific newly unrecognized medieval relationship is asserted.
