# E75 — a refrain is not a physical join

2 November 2026. Source readings are reference-exposed AI interpretations, not independent transcriptions.

## Test

The lower part of the retained [Fr 8073 capture 1 thumbnail](data/images/mpo-8073.jpg) provisionally shows the responsory *Funde vinum desolatis*, its verse *Liberalis liberali ... qui paratus est largiri ... plus quam postulas*, and the cue *Ut de*. The top of [capture 2](research/continuation_52/images/Fr8073-capture-2-native.jpg) appears to show *Gloria patri et filio et spiritui sancto*, followed by a second *Ut de*, before the hymn *Lucis huius festa*. See the [source regions](research/continuation_75/observations.json) for the precise coordinates and qualification. Capture 1 is only 800 pixels wide, so individual letters, the fold and portions of text remain difficult to check.

The [Cantus Index feast listing](https://cantusindex.org/feast/14072610?page=3) identifies *Funde vinum* as 600932 and *Liberalis liberali* as its verse 600932a; the indexed text has *Ut de regno claritatis* in the responsory. The search-indexed [Spiš antiphonal inventory](https://cantus.sk/source/6777?antibot_key=&page=7&string=) lists that responsory, its *Liberalis* verse and a *Gloria patri* verse in sequence. It identifies the latter as 600932za, a special Anne doxology: the full words on Fr 8073 have not been established, and that identification is **not** assigned to the local text. The inventory page could not be opened in the web reader after its search excerpt was returned; the source manuscript itself was not inspected. The [published AH 5 no. 34 text](https://de.scribd.com/document/66836506/Dreves-Analecta-Hymnica-Medii-Aevi-1886-Volume-V) also prints the responsory and *Liberalis* verse together.

The two *Ut de* cues are in different visible settings: one follows the verse ending in capture 1, and another follows the apparent doxology in capture 2. They are consistent with repeated instructions to return to the responsory; treating them as duplicated photographs of the same ink would be unsafe. The two photos may contain consecutive stages of one service, but the surviving cues alone do not establish a shared photographed patch, direct adjacency, the amount hidden at the fold, or original side identity. Repetition of text or inclusion of material not visible remains possible. This is an additional **candidate textual sequence**, not a proved overlap or a join of different fragments.

The bottom of capture 3 ends with the already identified *Felix terra* antiphon and a *Magnificat* cue, while capture 4 begins the known hymn *De stella sol oriturus*. These different texts do not supply a matching-ink overlap; no immediate 3→4 adjacency is inferred. The end of capture 4 and opening of capture 1 do not provide a verified match either. In particular, the [published hymn text](https://bibliotekanauki.pl/articles/1044076.pdf#page=24) continues the *Praesta laudanda trinitas da* doxology; that continuation has **not** been identified in capture 1. Similarity of service context is not a substitute for a visible seam.

## Result and next discriminating test

The new, tentative 1→2 textual context complements the stronger E74 split-word evidence for 2→3. Neither maps recto/verso or establishes another fragment's membership. The most useful next test is to inspect an authenticated Anne passage on an existing Ant 49 candidate, or identify independently visible matching material across the folds without re-requesting the previously failed native capture. Compare exact words and music, using other books containing the same responsory as controls. If the necessary correspondence is not preserved, record that dependency instead of inferring a physical join. No new manuscript image was downloaded, no book decision was changed and no discovery or independent confirmation is claimed.
