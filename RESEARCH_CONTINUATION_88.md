# E88: Published laboratory measurements for the two Finnish groups

**2 November 2026.** The previous turn E87 made bounded progress by establishing the date-range limits of an existing reuse study. This turn checks a different, newly located source bearing directly on the same manuscripts.

## The exact published evidence

Kasso and colleagues, [“A Glance to the Fragmenta Membranea Manuscript Collection through FTIR and Radiocarbon Analyses”](https://doi.org/10.1017/RDC.2022.81), *Radiocarbon* 65 (2023), 155–171, sampled three leaves under each catalogue shelfmark. Their Table 2 (p.162) includes **IV.156 f.8**, the exact Helsinki leaf needed for the Fr 10370 opening comparison. It also includes **IV.157 ff.2 and 7**, which are relevant to existing Turku C and Fr 10370 placement questions. A sampled leaf is not an image of its writing, and the study does not settle those associations.

## What this means for a same-book test

There is striking spread even within the published IV.156 group: the reported age for f.8 differs by 159 radiocarbon years from f.21. Conversely IV.156 f.8 and IV.157 f.2 differ by 19 radiocarbon years. These are arithmetic differences between reported central values, **not a statistical test or a comparison of calendar-date distributions**. A medieval book uses different animal skins and the published catalogue groupings are not independently proved by their labels. Neither small cross-group differences nor large within-group differences establish same-book or different-book membership.

The authors state that four of these six results yield dates younger than expected on historical and palaeographic grounds. They discuss possible younger carbon contamination and fire-related damage, while explicitly treating the mechanism as tentative and acknowledging other possible contributors. Thus the measurements are published primary laboratory evidence, but **are not a clean independent identity fingerprint** for the two books. The similarity of contamination patterns does not establish shared fire history, storage, production, or a physical join.
