## What we could see

The Vatican catalogue's ordinary search led to the named manuscript and its image viewer. Starting at the previously identified f. 125v and tracing backwards through labelled folios, we found an earlier candidate opening on **f. 122r**: a red heading apparently referring to St Catherine followed by a large red initial and *Inclita*. The legible text appears to continue with the Catherine antiphon reported in the literature. On **f. 125v**, a separate red Catherine/virgin heading is followed by another, abbreviated *Inclita* incipit. The exact wording of both headings and the expansion of the second incipit require further checking.

This is visual support for the narrow point made in [Grajewski 2025, p. 78 note 11](https://www.spmk.pl/files/roczniki/spmk20/06_-Grajewski_-Inclita.pdf): two Catherine office variants in this breviary begin with the same shortened antiphon. The study already reported that fact, so the project is checking prior work rather than claiming a discovery. We did not collate both full offices or establish that the same complete service rubric was repeated.

The [Heidelberg catalogue's indexed description](https://digi.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/de/bpd/beschreibungen/bav_pal_lat_527.html) places the end of the sanctoral at Catherine on 125v–126r and reports the common of saints beginning on 126r. The viewed upper 126r has a large subsequent red heading apparently for an apostles' vigil, consistent with that transition. The catalogue's brief endpoint description should not be read as saying that preceding folios contain no Catherine material. Its dating and account of alternating hands remain attributed catalogue observations; we did not verify hand assignments.

## Why it matters to the Swedish fragments

The visible example shows why a second opening chant alone cannot be treated as an automatic proof of two parent books. **It does not establish that Fr 8304 and Fr 20106 were in the same book or overturn the published exclusion.** The target argument concerns a more specific repeated heading and fragment sequence; Fr 20106 remains uninspected, the Vatican book is a breviary rather than the target antiphonary, and alternatives such as compilation and multiple hands remain open.
