# E77 — a conditional difference in dedication chant order

2 November 2026. This is an exploratory, reference-exposed comparison of two already retained fragments. It was interrupted and resumed before recording. No independent reader has checked the Latin or the ordering.

**Later E78 qualification:** the small Fr 10437 cue is plausibly a verse and response. Other indexed books contain both that verse and a full *Domus mea* antiphon, including one whose first two antiphons match the tentative Fr 10437 order. See [E78](RESEARCH_CONTINUATION_78.md); neither full local omission nor original book separation is established.

## What the photographs show

The same dedication antiphon, *Domum tuam domine*, appears provisionally on [Fr 8066 capture 2](research/continuation_44/images/Fr8066-capture-2-native.jpg) and [Fr 10437 capture 1](research/continuation_54/images/Fr10437-capture-1-native.jpg). In Fr 8066, a red *lau/des* heading precedes the antiphon. Its end appears to break *die- / rum* across captures 2 and 3. On [capture 3](research/continuation_44/images/Fr8066-capture-3-native.jpg), *Domus mea domus orationis vocabitur* follows the *rum* and the psalm-ending cue, then a new antiphon begins *Hec est domus domini*. That split and ordering were already recorded in E44–E45.

On Fr 10437 capture 1, after the visible *Domum tuam* antiphon and a provisional *D[ominu]s reg[n...]* psalm cue with *Euouae*, the next visible antiphon starts *Hec est domus domini*. No separate *Domus mea* antiphon appears between those visible items in this selected field. The source crop contains nearby small writing and rubrics, so its precise reading order and service context require checking. A red *In laudibus* rubric is visible nearby; its placement and relationship to the larger notated line should not be treated as an independently established full service map. The [annotated image regions](research/continuation_77/observations.json) identify what can be checked directly.

Thus, **if** the Fr 8066 *die- / rum* continuation is correct and the selected Fr 10437 field is read in this order, their local sequences differ: 8066 has an intervening *Domus mea* antiphon, while 10437 does not in that visible position. This is not a claim that 10437 lacks *Domus mea* elsewhere; capture 2 includes a small *Domus me...* cue whose full function is not determined here. Do not normalize that cue as another complete antiphon.

## Check against other sources and competing explanations

The [Cantus Network Salzburg edition](https://gams.uni-graz.at/o:cantus.salzburg.vo/sdef:TEI/get?locale=en&mode=view:edition), *De dedicatione*, lists the three antiphons in the same order as the tentative Fr 8066 sequence. A [Cantus inventory of Paris, Latin 1085, f. 65v](https://cantusdatabase.org/chant/447844/) also lists them as the first three Lauds antiphons. The latter was read from the search-indexed inventory; its direct open returned a cache miss, and its manuscript image was not inspected. These are comparative textual descriptions, not an independent reading of either Swedish fragment. A limited search for both fragment identifiers together did not locate a relevant direct comparison; it is not an exhaustive literature or novelty audit.

The MPO catalogue assigns Fr 8066 and Fr 10437 to different reconstructed codices, 1146 and 1505 respectively. Those are **source assignments**, not independently established book identities or a calibrated different-book control. The apparent order difference compares two occurrences, not two proven complete services. Repetition of the same antiphon, different liturgical sections or occasions, abbreviation, an unexpected capture order, or a composite book could produce differing local sequences without proving separate parent books. The selected Fr 10437 field should be read independently before asserting a decisive omission. Exact note-by-note melodies, matching pitches, and chronology were not compared.

This is a cross-family **comparison control using existing objects**, not a new relationship candidate or a change to the frozen queue. If confirmed, it would show why a common chant incipit is insufficient and provide a specific service-sequence feature to test against the original targets. It does not resolve the Fr 8066/9620 or Finnish/10437 memberships and is not established as a new scientific discovery. No source image was downloaded, historical decision changed, or external contact made.
