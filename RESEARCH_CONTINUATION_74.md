The [two annotations](research/continuation_74/observations.json) identify the original image regions.

## Check against known versions

The passage belongs to *Lucis huius festa*, already identified at work level in the MPO record as **Analecta Hymnica 52 no. 101**. On the two photographs the provisional stanza openings run: *Lucis huius festa* → *Mater matris Christi* → *Annam sic expresse* → *Ex hac carnis planta* → *Celo ... sublata* → *Trino laus et uni*. This sequence also appears in the [1909 edition](https://de.scribd.com/doc/66837361/Dreves-Analecta-Hymnica-Medii-Aevi-1886-Volume-LII), no. 101, and in the [Cantus inventory of Zutphen manuscript 6, f. 246r](https://cantusdatabase.org/chant/533829/). The latter was visible in search-indexed source navigation; its manuscript image was not examined. Thus this stanza order is not unique to Fr 8073, and neither source establishes that the books are related.

A [published transcription of the Tolnai breviary](https://mez.egyhazzene.hu/pdf/11/11_23_229236.pdf), Áron Kelemen, *Tolnai Máté breviáriuma*, 2003/2004, prints the same hymn in a different stanza order: *Ex hac* follows *Lucis*, while *Annam* occurs later. This tests the assumption that every witness has the same order. It is a printed-text comparator, not a newly inspected medieval manuscript or a complete survey of variants.

In capture 3, the fifth stanza provisionally reads *Celo tam sublata mulier sancta ... soluat a reatu*. The 1909 base text has *Caelo iam sublata mulier beata ... purget a reatu*. The reading needs verification; its prior occurrence, distribution and significance are unestablished. The 1909 edition's apparatus and its uncollated sources must not be treated as an exhaustive negative search. No claim of a unique or newly discovered variant follows.
