# E71 — a conditional page-order test for Fr 20106 and Fr 8201

2 November 2026. This is a test design, not a finding that the fragments conflict.

## Source statements

Grajewski, [*Offices of St. Catherine of Alexandria* (2022), p. 293](https://doi.org/10.25167/ls.4818), associates Fr 20106 and Fr 8201, citing the continuation of a Catherine antiphon: *Specie corporis ... sed fide* in 20106 and *pulchrior sponso ...* in 8201. The same article separates Fr 8304 on other grounds. The local 8201 image was examined in [E65](RESEARCH_CONTINUATION_65.md); we have not inspected Fr 20106.

The [local MPO catalogue export](research/mpo.csv), Fr 20106 row, reports one leaf and lists Clement, Catherine, Barbara, and the hymn *Novo decurrens lumine lascivit sol in virgine*. The pinned BOMPAC workbook repeats this description; it is not independent confirmation. [Cantus Index](https://cantusindex.org/genre/139?page=8) identifies that hymn with Barbara (830225). Neither the catalogue list nor the comparative index establishes the actual order, location, sides, physical completeness, or text of the Swedish fragment. In particular, the description is not a side-by-side transcription.

## What would create a contradiction?

Suppose primary evidence eventually showed all of the following: (1) 20106 is one intact original leaf read in the ordinary order; (2) its Catherine antiphon prefix occurs before its Barbara hymn; (3) 8201 contains the immediate missing continuation of that occurrence; and (4) that continuation must precede the Barbara hymn in this particular sequence. Then the required order would be **20106 prefix → 8201 continuation → 20106 Barbara**. An intact 20106 leaf cannot have an intact 8201 leaf inserted between its two sides. That particular set of assumptions would be inconsistent.

None of those crucial physical-order premises is established by the catalogue list alone. In particular, the record may summarize rather than order contents; it does not map material to sides, and a fragment or folded bifolium must not be silently treated as an intact single original leaf. A non-adjacent text concordance is also not itself proof of literal physical continuity. There may be other explanations, including a mistaken description, a different ordering or repeated material. A bifolium with intervening leaves provides a logical counterexample to applying the intact-single-leaf rule without checking the object.

## Discriminating next step

Locate securely identified views of **both sides of Fr 20106** or a detailed institutional description. Record physical extent, original recto/verso and the positions and literal readings of the Catherine and Barbara passages. Compare the relevant edge with Fr 8201 and determine whether the text really breaks at a leaf boundary or is merely a textual continuation across some unobserved gap. If the Barbara passage precedes the Catherine passage, is absent, or belongs to differently arranged material, this specific conditional objection does not apply. Even if it applies, re-examine the assumptions before changing a historical attribution.
