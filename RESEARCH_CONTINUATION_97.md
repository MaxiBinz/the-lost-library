# E97: faint marks qualify the Fr 8994 boundary reading

**3 November 2026.** The preceding E96 turn added an exploratory measurement to an existing candidate. This pass returns to the more consequential Fr 8994 / IV.156 boundary. It finds a limitation in the earlier source description, not a new manuscript attribution.

## Observation

On [Fr 8994 capture 4](data/images/mpo-8994-capture-4-1808px.jpg), pale ochre marks appear to the right of the clearly readable *tempus est* in the final photographed text row. Their shapes could be letter strokes. A tentative *ut* is visually conceivable, but the reading is **not established**: the observer already knew that *ut* was expected next from the reference text, creating a serious risk of seeing the expected word. Nor is it established that the marks are original running text, a cue, a later addition, ink showing through, offset, staining or an image artifact. The unaltered source, precise region and alternatives are in [the annotation](research/continuation_97/observations.json).

[E40](RESEARCH_CONTINUATION_40.md) described the visible row as ending *tempus est* and used the catalogue's reported *ut laborum* opening for IV.156 ff.9–11 as a conditional continuation test. It is more precise to say that *tempus est* is the **last clearly transcribed word**, rather than treating the area to its right as blank or the physical end as established. E40 already warned that capture and original-side boundaries were unresolved. The Finnish image was not inspected; no overlapping word, repeated text, immediate continuation, adjacency or physical join is established.

The [Hill Museum & Manuscript Library glossary](https://hmmlschool.org/lexicon/14466/) explains that some medieval catchwords repeat the following page's opening word. This is a **general alternative**, not evidence that these marks are a catchword: their form, position, chronology and function remain unknown. The concordance's common wording cannot settle the reading. The known suggested Codex 1505 / IV.156 association remains prior work, not a finding of this pass.

## Next discriminating test

A competent independent reading of the unaltered source, preferably alongside an authenticated image of the other face or the physical object, could test whether the marks are ink on the current surface and whether letters are present. An authorized, source-identified IV.156 f.9 image is separately needed to inspect its actual opening, notation, side and signature. A confident reading of either source would not by itself establish the two pages belong together.
