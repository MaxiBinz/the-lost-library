# E64: where to look for the missing Fr 20106

**Result:** A 103 is not a unique pointer to the psalter previously found in the National Library catalogue. A researcher who inventoried Swedish manuscripts reports a separate National Library fragment collection deposited at the National Archives under the old designation A 103. That makes the deposited collection a plausible place to look for Fr 20106's image, but **does not yet identify the exact fragment, its current location or an image**.

The pinned MPO metadata describes Fr 20106 as KB, A 103, k 95, with an antiphonal containing Catherine material. The separately catalogued [National Library A 103](https://www.manuscripta.se/ms/101111) is a 36-leaf fragmented psalter, dated by its catalogue to around the turn of the thirteenth century. Its matching short shelfmark is not enough to identify the missing fragment.

[The inventory report by Eva Lindqvist Sandgren](https://www.rj.se/bidrag/2002/illuminerade-handskrifter-i-svenska-samlingar/) describes the old A 103 as a separate National Library fragment deposit at the National Archives during her 2003–2006 work. As an additional reference-pattern check, [Anne Reese's 2005/2006 Uppsala dissertation](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2%3A419695/FULLTEXT01.pdf), printed pp. 46–47 and 53, lists a **different** fragment, Fr 20120 / A 103 k 121, under the National Archives, with a KB versus ARKIS/RA distinction. Previously, E51 also documented the A 103 k 118 pattern on Fr 20274. These are related but distinct objects: they must not be merged.

The connection of **Fr 20106 itself** to that deposited collection is a project inference from the full metadata and historical context, not an explicit identification in those two sources. Cataloguing and custody may have changed. No source here supplies a photograph of Fr 20106 or proves whether it comes from the same book as Fr 8201. The published different-book conclusion about Fr 8304 remains attributed to its author; the local source recheck is still incomplete. This is a useful archival clarification, not a new medieval manuscript discovery.

The highest-value next test is a current institutional record or explicit reproduction identifying **Fr 20106 / A 103 k 95**, followed by a direct check of the Catherine opening and continuity. [Sources and access outcomes](research/continuation_64/sources.json) preserve the exact scope; no image was downloaded, no historical decision changed and no one was contacted. One additional indexed Alvin result failed to open and was inadvertently fetched again by a `find` operation in the same web call; neither response supplied usable evidence, and the route must not be retried.
