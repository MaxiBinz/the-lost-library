# E57: Fr 8311 chant context and a published parallel

**E58 update:** Subsequent indexed searches located two other manuscript folio lists with all six candidate chant/verse identifiers in the same order. See [E58](RESEARCH_CONTINUATION_58.md). The Trier-specific first-verse difference below remains correct for that comparator; it does not make the six-ID pattern unique.

1 November 2026. Four previously retained Riksarkivet photographs of Fr 8311 were inspected again. Their order in the capture filenames is not an established map of the original medieval sides. The reading was made after earlier exposure to the images and some reference results; it is neither blind nor independently confirmed. The observations and their exact source-image coordinates are in [observations.json](research/continuation_57/observations.json), and the protocol, source hashes and full limitations are in [continuation_57](research/continuation_57/).

The first image provisionally reads **Dum deambularet** and its **Vocem tuam** verse (candidate Cantus 006537 and 006537a). The second contains part of **In sudore vultus tui** (candidate 006937). The third begins with words consistent with the ending of its **Pro eo** verse (candidate 006937a), followed on that same image by **Ubi est Abel frater tuus** (candidate 007804). The fourth contains words consistent with its **Maledicta terra** verse (candidate 007804a), followed by a **Miserere mei deus** opening (candidate 003774). These are selected text readings, not complete transcriptions. Continuation between capture 2 and capture 3 is plausible, but their filenames do not prove physical adjacency; the complete order and original side mapping remain unverified.

The published [Trier manuscript inventory](https://epub.uni-regensburg.de/51986/3/Historiae%20Trevirenses_Appendix_Brink.pdf), printed p. 29, indexes Trier Bistumsarchiv Ms. 480, fols. 51v–52r, with the three candidate responsories in succession and a following Miserere, including Pro eo and Maledicta terra verses. It gives **006537b** for the first verse, whereas our Fr 8311 reading suggests **006537a**. The published inventory therefore shows a relevant existing combination, not an identical complete sequence. The web-reader text was inspected; an attempted PDF screenshot failed, so the visual page layout was not verified. Neither the Trier original nor its music was inspected. This selected comparison does not measure rarity or establish copying, book identity, physical joins, melodic agreement or a novel finding.

Five direct Cantus entries failed to open, and are recorded in [results.json](research/continuation_57/results.json) as failed routes rather than consulted pages. No new source files were acquired. The 208-file working image inventory and 1,675,860,841-byte conservative transfer plan, including the existing historical reserve, are unchanged; exact web traffic is not measured. All lead decisions and the frozen 2E/7C/1D case review remain unchanged. A useful next identity test would require an independently checkable, discriminating text variant, physical feature or original-side map, not simply the presence of common chants.
