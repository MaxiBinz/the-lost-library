# E69 — an existing counterexample to a general premise

2 November 2026. Exploratory source re-reading after seeing the argument and comparative texts; not blind or independently reviewed.

## A published counterexample with limits

Grajewski's 2022 article assigns Fr 8304 to a different book from Fr 20106/8201. Part of that argument is that a Catherine office cannot begin twice in one antiphonary. In a [later 2025 article, p. 78 note 11](https://www.spmk.pl/files/roczniki/spmk20/06_-Grajewski_-Inclita.pdf), the **same author** reports a compilatory breviary, Vatican **Pal. lat. 527**, with two Catherine offices, *Ave virgo speciosa* and *Inclita sanctae virginis*, both beginning with the same shortened *Inclita* antiphon. This is already-published evidence, not a discovery by our project.

It makes a universal ban on repeated Catherine-office opening chants unsafe. It **does not refute the target exclusion**: the reported offices are different variants; the 2025 note does not establish that the same explicit service heading appears twice. A breviary with multiple hands may differ from the target antiphonary, and we have not seen the Vatican original or Fr 20106. The existing published different-book decision remains attributed to its author; the exact inference requires the missing original passages and consideration of compilation, codicological structure and alternatives. The author is not an independent reviewer of our project.

The [Heidelberg catalogue](https://digi.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/de/bpd/beschreibungen/bav_pal_lat_527.html) indexes Pal. lat. 527 as a Speyer breviary and reports at least two alternating hands. The catalogue information available in the search extract does not establish the relation of those hands to the two offices. Its linked facsimile did not open through the web reader. Three catalogue find requests accidentally repeated the same failed fetch; this is recorded in [sources](research/continuation_69/sources.json) and must not be repeated.

## Correcting our earlier reading

Re-examination of the existing 1000-pixel Fr 8304 capture 4 suggests that E49's *sollempnitas* was our transcription error. The candidate is **sollempni[a?]**, followed by *suscipiat alacriter pia ma[…]*. The last letter and ending are uncertain; no word is supplied beyond what is visible. This reading was informed by external chant texts and has not been independently checked. The earlier record is retained with a visible supersession note, and the [new image-linked annotation](research/continuation_69/observations.json) retains the image checksum and exact region. A single request for the manifest-linked higher-resolution version failed local DNS; it was not retried.

[Cantus](https://cantusdatabase.org/chant/205358/) indexes a comparable longer antiphon as ID 202513 in Cambrai 38 f.378v. The database entry is contextual, not a direct reading of the Cambrai manuscript or a book-identity test. E49's tentative *Inclita* identification remains provisional and the correction is a project transcription correction, not a new medieval variant.
