2 November 2026. This is a reference-exposed AI reading of a retained photograph, not an independent transcription.

## What the photograph shows

On [Fr 10437 capture 2](research/continuation_54/images/Fr10437-capture-2-native.jpg), the visible field provisionally contains the end of *Lapides pretiosi*, followed by a short, unnotated two-line cue *[V?] Domus me[...] / [marker?] Domus or[...]*. Nearby a red rubric provisionally reads *Ad bened[ictus] ant[iphona]* and introduces a large *Mane surgens Iacob* opening. The [annotation](research/continuation_78/observations.json) points to the full local context. The markers and abbreviated endings have not been independently checked; no unprinted words are silently supplied as a literal reading.

The [Slovak Early Music Database inventory](https://cantus.sk/source/33906) supplies an external example of *Domus mea* followed by *Domus orationis* as a verse and response, Cantus 008036. It is a plausible **work/genre identification** for the Fr 10437 cue, not a proof that the Swedish words or their markers have been read correctly. The database's example is from a different source and position. Its search-indexed record was read; a direct web-reader open failed, and no comparator manuscript image was inspected.

## A strong counterexample to an omission inference

The [Cantus inventory of Kraków, Carmelite Convent, Ms. 3, f. 80r](https://cantusdatabase.org/chant/319875/) lists the Lauds antiphons in the order *Domum tuam* → *Haec est domus* → *Domus mea* → *Bene fundata* → *Lapides pretiosi*. It then lists a hymn, the *Domus mea* verse (008036), and the *Mane surgens* Benedictus antiphon. This is a **published index**, not an original manuscript examination. The direct Cantus page returned a cache miss; the indexed excerpt supplied the complete folio sequence.

The Kraków list supplies a concrete alternative to assuming the small Fr 10437 cue replaces a full *Domus mea* antiphon: another book lists both, with the full antiphon **after** *Haec est domus*. Its opening two antiphons are also compatible with the tentative Fr 10437 ordering in E77. Kraków includes an intervening hymn in the later part of its list, so the comparison is an ordered-context parallel, not an assertion of identical adjacency or complete sequence. The fold and photo sequencing in Fr 10437 leave room for further text to be checked; the earlier local observation does not prove which, if any, additional items occurred there.

An [Utrecht Ms. 406 inventory](https://cantusdatabase.org/chant/496650) independently lists a full *Domus mea* antiphon before *Haec est domus* and a *Domus mea* verse later, before *Mane surgens*. It therefore supplies a second arrangement where an antiphon and verse share the incipit. Only the indexed inventory was read; it is not an independent reading of Fr 10437. The search-exposed Utrecht and Kraków inventories are separate manuscript descriptions, while their repeated database records are not additional manuscripts.

## Implication

E77's **visible local next-antiphon difference remains conditional**; it is not evidence of a whole-office omission, a different parent book, a unique usage, or a Kraków/Utrecht connection. The exact Fr 10437 capture-to-side map, the hidden portion around the fold, the actual marker readings and the earlier Fr 8066 split remain open.
