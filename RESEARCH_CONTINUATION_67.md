# E67 — a historical candidate for the Sziget breviary

2 November 2026. Exploratory, after reading the matching descriptions; not blind or preregistered.

## What the sources say

Toldy's 1855 edition, introduction p. IX, describes a Sziget breviary written in 1399 and refers to István Szilágyi's account of Máramarossziget. On p. XIII he attributes his quotation from the Sziget breviary to Szilágyi. That edited quotation includes the `virgo … beata` and `vaga` wording provisionally read on Fr 8201 in E66. This is already printed prior art; the 1850/1851 Szilágyi account and the medieval source have not been read. [Toldy](https://mek.oszk.hu/17000/17077/17077.pdf).

In 1942 Asztrik Gábriel described a fourteenth-century breviary, historical **no. 167**, deposited from the Máramarossziget Lyceum at the Debrecen library. He reports a colophon, at his f. 313, recording completion in 1399. The article cautions that its folio numbers are often private and may need later correction. It does not give the selected Catherine text or explicitly cross-reference Toldy's book. [Gábriel](https://epa.oszk.hu/00000/00021/00211/pdf/MKSZ_EPA00021_1942_66_04_00211.pdf).

## What follows, and what does not

The matching date, type of book and historic Sziget association make no. 167 a reasonable **candidate** for the book behind Toldy's quotation. This is our inference from already observed descriptions. They could describe different books; errors, inherited reports or later collection changes are also possible. No independent collation has been made. Neither no. 167 nor f. 313 is a verified present-day identifier, and historic possession does not show where the book was made. No medieval manuscript image was inspected. This is not evidence that the Sziget book and Fr 8201 are the same physical book or descended directly from one another.

The earlier Szilágyi publication itself remains unread: Toldy cites it as 1850, whereas an [indexed 1861 academy almanac](https://real-j.mtak.hu/14657/1/MTA_Almanach_1861.pdf) lists the article as 1851.
