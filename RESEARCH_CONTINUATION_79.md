# E79 — a first musical comparison of the same antiphon

2 November 2026. This is a post-selection, reference-exposed AI reading, not a blind or independent transcription. It compares two existing objects as an exploratory cross-family control, without adding a historical same-book candidate to the frozen queue.

**Later E80 context:** the next two syllables, *do-mi* of *domini*, provisionally descend in both fragments. This is a separate unreviewed local observation; the two wider sensitivity bounds leave the Fr 8066 direction unresolved. See [E80](RESEARCH_CONTINUATION_80.md).

## The same words, different apparent movement

The [selected Fr 10437 image](research/continuation_54/images/Fr10437-capture-1-native.jpg) and [Fr 8066 image](research/continuation_44/images/Fr8066-capture-3-native.jpg) both provisionally notate *Hec est domus domini firmiter ...* as an antiphon. In the first four apparent noteheads aligned to *Hec / est / do- / mus*, the Fr 10437 third head appears below its second, then the fourth returns higher. The Fr 8066 heads appear approximately level over the same syllables. This is an **apparent local contour contrast**, not a completed melody or an absolute-pitch reading. [Source regions](research/continuation_79/observations.json) and [approximate source-pixel centres](research/continuation_79/readings.json) make the comparison inspectable.

The two images use different note shapes, the selected points and underlay are manually assigned, and the red rules are only approximate local orientation references. For *est* → *do-*, a reproducible sensitivity calculation tests ±3, ±4 and ±5 pixels per selected centre plus a slope allowance. Fr 10437's apparent downward movement remains positive in these illustrative ranges; Fr 8066's movement remains unresolved. To compare images at different scales, the calculation expresses displacement in approximate local staff spacings, allowing ±2 pixels on each spacing. The resulting possible between-source differences include zero, so this is **not a robustly established interval contrast** under those assumptions. These bounds are not measured errors or confidence intervals; no automatic or independently reviewed note recognition was performed. See [comparison.json](research/continuation_79/comparison.json).

## Published musical controls

The [Cantus entry for Klosterneuburg CCl 1012, f. 77r](https://cantusdatabase.org/chant/285848/) identifies the same antiphon, 002998, at Lauds and encodes its first four syllables as `d d c d`: same, down, up. The [Cantus entry for Einsiedeln 611, f. 271r](https://cantusdatabase.org/chant/233469) encodes the same antiphon's first four as `d d d d`: level. These are search-indexed **transcriptions of different manuscript witnesses**, accessed after the local reading; the original comparator images were not inspected. The contours are compatible with the respective visual candidates, but absolute pitches were not assigned to the Swedish photographs and the complete melodies were not collated. Thus a possible contrast of this sort is already documented elsewhere and is not by itself a unique variant, source relationship, identity test or discovery.

The source catalogues assign Fr 10437 and Fr 8066 to different reconstructed codices, but these assignments have not been independently proven as a difficult negative. Different services, variant tradition, copying practices, notation or transcription error remain alternatives. A real same-book/different-book test requires adjudicated same-work transcriptions and established positive and negative book controls. The earlier Fr 10437 fold and Fr 8066 reading order remain unresolved. No historical lead decision or frozen classification changed, no new manuscript image was acquired, and no independent confirmation or novelty claim is made.
