2 November 2026. The Turku fragment offers a small, source-testable **possible catalogue correction**. Codices Fennici transcribes the antiphon beginning *Sicut mirra* with the word **redisti**. In the [manuscript viewer](https://www.codicesfennici.fi/items/show/249), the initial character of that word appears instead to be **d**, making **dedisti** a plausible reading. The museum record repeats the Codices Fennici wording and credits that source; it is not an independent transcription.

## How to inspect it

Select the second viewer image, `TMMA.FragC_0002_v.jpg`, and zoom in on the bottom notated line of the **left** writing field. The catalogue calls this folio 1v. In the observed viewer state, the source-image rectangle was `xywh=1630,3047,922,642`. The viewer reset an attempted deep link to the first image in this session, so select the second image manually if necessary. The photograph is credited to Mikko Kyynäräinen / Turun Museokeskus; the CF page labels its images CC BY-ND 4.0. No image or cropped derivative was saved.

The contested word follows *odorem*. Its first character appears to have a tall, left-leaning ascender and a bowl like the **d** in *odorem* and the later **d** in the same word. The final strokes are faded, so the cautious project reading is **dedi[sti?]**. The [Utrecht Ms. 406 transcription in Cantus](https://cantusdatabase.org/chant/493230/) gives *dedisti* for the same antiphon, Cantus 004942. That supplies context and an alternative to test; it does not transcribe the Turku page and must not supply letters that cannot be seen.

The visual reading was made after seeing the catalogue and a concordance, so it is reference-assisted, unblinded and not independently reviewed. A mistaken interpretation of the local script or an editorial convention remains possible. Exact-phrase web searches did not establish an earlier notice of the issue; incomplete indexing cannot establish novelty. First notice remains unknown.
