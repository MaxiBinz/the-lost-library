# E114 — the current combined CSV carries the same field discrepancy

**Published-impact check, 4 November:** A targeted search for the dataset title, DOI, and column/layout terminology did not identify a published analysis that demonstrably used the affected `Columns` field. Search-indexed text from Eskola's 2025 [dataset introduction](https://www.fragmentology.ms/article/download/RN_Eskola_08/8737/24725), previously read in E22, describes coverage of dating, genre, type, origin, size and reuse; indexed figure text describes results by dating and origin. This does not establish that the paper or any other study used the affected field, nor does it prove that no affected analysis exists. A direct web-reader open of `https://doi.org/10.24446/no5r` in this pass returned inaccessible; that exact route must not be retried. The existing [E110](RESEARCH_CONTINUATION_110.md) local substitution is a controlled demonstration of possible impact, not evidence of an error in someone else's publication. Avoid further generic impact searches without a specific paper or analysis to check.

**4 November 2026.** This is a reproducible data-quality observation, not a medieval book attribution. In Zenodo's [combined BOMPAC v1.1 record](https://zenodo.org/records/19046496), the version list showed v1 and v1.1, with v1.1 dated 16 March 2026 and listed first. The publisher says v1.1 adds unchanged CSV data. We separately downloaded its 18,526,969-byte CSV once; its MD5 matches the publisher's displayed checksum and its SHA-256 is in the [receipt](research/continuation_114/download_receipt.json). The listed spreadsheet MD5 matches our previously audited workbook. This is a check of the combined record's displayed version list and files, not a fresh latest-version check for the separate raw dataset or evidence that no correction exists outside Zenodo.

The [offline script](research/continuation_114/audit.py) compared all **29,586 data rows** in CSV and workbook by row order and unique `Index`. After blank/None, numeric-cell and surrounding-whitespace normalization, it found **zero differences in each of seven fields**: `Index`, `Fr_number`, `Fr_reference`, `Database`, `Row_exclusion`, `No_of_leaves`, and `Columns`. It does not compare the other fields. For the 22,553 included MPO records, both CSV count fields are nonnegative integer strings on **22,248**, and the two values agree on **all 22,248**. This exactly reproduces the relevant numeric pattern in the spreadsheet [E108](RESEARCH_CONTINUATION_108.md); the new CSV and old workbook are two representations of the same dataset, not independent historical evidence.

The earlier exact-join source check found 5,598 disagreements among 14,415 eligible raw-versus-combined 1/2 comparisons, and E111 identified 6,543 separate blank-original cases. Because the seven checked fields match on every indexed row, those selected comparisons apply to these CSV fields as well, subject to the same matching, denominator and raw-source limits. This does **not** verify each physical layout, establish the cause, establish first notice, or prove the raw catalogue correct. No independent reviewer or maintainer has assessed it. The duplicated-count hypothesis remains strong but unconfirmed; no public correction or outside message was sent.

Reproduce with `.venv/bin/python research/continuation_114/audit.py`; the check verifies both file hashes, full row count, unique indices, per-field equality and the integer-count pattern.
