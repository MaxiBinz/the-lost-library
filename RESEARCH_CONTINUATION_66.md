# E66: a textual variant in the Fr 8201 comparison

The clearer Fr 8201 image provisionally reads *virgo Katherina beata* and *vaga* in the responsory following the two antiphons examined in E65. The same three chant identifiers occur in the same order in a published transcription of another fragment, but that transcription gives different words at those points. This is an unblinded comparison, not a new book identification.

The Slovak National Museum published Eva Veselovská's transcription of **MUS I 12**, fols. 1r–1v, in its [2011 conference proceedings](https://www.snm.sk/swift_data/source/hudobne_muzeum/pdf_dokumenty/Hudobne_pramene_2011_Komplet.pdf), printed pp. 26–27. It lists *Specie corporis* (Cantus 204720), *Traditur ergo* (204938), then *Nobilis et pulchra* (601492). Its third chant has *prudens … puella* and *vana*, while the [Fr 8201 source region](research/continuation_66/observations.json) provisionally has *virgo … beata* and *vaga*. The manuscript image for MUS I 12 was not inspected: the comparison uses the author's published transcription. Ellipses around its side change leave any intervening material uncertain.

The variant wording itself is already recorded in [Toldy's 1855 edition](https://mek.oszk.hu/17000/17077/17077.pdf), which attributes it to an earlier account of a breviary; the underlying manuscript was not inspected. An indexed Karlsruhe manuscript gives a mixture of these readings, with *virgo … puella* and *vaga*. These are useful counterexamples to calling the wording unique. Whether this particular reading of **Fr 8201** has been previously discussed remains unverified.

The published proposed Fr 20106/8201 break lies **within** the first antiphon; the MUS I 12 transcription places its side change after that antiphon. It is therefore not a counterexample to that exact break, and it neither disproves nor confirms the proposed association. Same word order or variant does not establish a shared book, direct copying, geographic origin or melody.
